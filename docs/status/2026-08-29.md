# Status — 2026-08-29

## Decision point: the gate is decidable, nothing is promoted

Tasks 1–8 of this plan re-scored three closed or stalled representation-track
campaigns against a 96-held-out-light estimator instead of the 12-light draw
the gate originally ran on. The 96-light count was pre-registered before any
new *training*; these re-reads are re-reads of already-committed checkpoints,
not the pre-registered runs themselves. **No model was retrained.** Every
number below comes from `examples/rescore_checkpoints.py` reading the already-committed
`out/**/train/*.pt` checkpoints; the 12-light re-scores reproduce the original
`report.json` files exactly, to 6 decimal places, which is the evidence the
re-scorer isn't silently changing the estimand. Everything past this report — any seed added at
the next scheduled look — spends CPU-hours that Tasks 1–8 did not.

**Nothing here is a promotion.** The 96-light read moves means and shrinks the
seed count each gate needs, but every verdict recorded below is unchanged from
before the re-read: `continue`, `continue`, and *not promoted*. What improved is
the precision of the estimator, not the evidence for any encoder reaching
parity with `pixel2d`.

### 1. R1 Kitchen parity (`out/r1-parity-kitchen-eq/`)

12 → 96 held-out lights, same 8 seeds, same checkpoints:

| arm | mean Δ (12 lights) | mean Δ (96 lights) | seeds_needed (12) | seeds_needed (96) | verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `world_sparse` | −0.493 dB | −0.009 dB | 34 | 17 | `continue` |
| `world_normal_triplane` | −0.446 dB | −0.416 dB | 18 | 14 | `continue` |
| `world3d` | −1.368 dB | −0.674 dB | 73 | 25 | `continue` |

Evidence: `out/r1-parity-kitchen-eq/rescore-96.json` vs `rescore-12.json`.
**All three verdicts remain `continue` — nothing is promoted.** The 12-light
numbers are retracted only as measurements of the arms; they remain accurate
records of what a 12-light estimator saw, and are not edited.

### 2. K1 resolution sweep (`out/r1-kitchen-parity-k1-eq/`)

Same re-read applied to the `world_sparse` finest-resolution sweep (32/48/64/96/128),
same 8 seeds:

| finest_resolution | mean Δ (12 lights) | mean Δ (96 lights) | seeds_needed (12) | seeds_needed (96) | verdict (96) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 32 | −0.409 dB | −0.025 dB | 46 | 13 | `continue` |
| 48 | −0.248 dB | +0.036 dB | 22 | 16 | `continue` |
| 64 | −0.580 dB | +0.021 dB | 19 | 9 | `pass` |
| 96 | −0.381 dB | +0.138 dB | 47 | 19 | `continue` |
| 128 | −0.493 dB | −0.009 dB | 34 | 17 | `continue` |

Every mean collapsed toward zero at 96 lights. The Spearman correlation between
resolution and mean delta — the signal the K1 rung existed to test — flips from
ρ = −0.30 at 12 lights to **ρ = +0.30 at 96 lights**, computed on the identical
five checkpoints; a sign flip on the same data at *p* ≈ 0.68 (n = 5) is
consistent with 8-seed noise, not a resolved trend. **K1 stays undecided; K2–K4
remain cancelled** (their gating condition — K1 confirming the predicted
direction — is unmet).

`finest64`'s marginal `pass` is not evidence of parity: it has only the
third-best mean of the five resolutions, passes because it happens to have the
smallest between-seed sd at 8 seeds, and fails under a pooled-sd estimate. Carry
that caveat forward — it is a fragile pass, not a result to build on.

the margin the campaign needed shrank, not the gap.

## What this changes and what it doesn't

- **Changes:** how many seeds each open question needs before a verdict is
  trustworthy. At 96 lights the R1-parity and K1 seed counts needed to decide
  are roughly half what the 12-light estimator implied.
- **Does not change:** any verdict. `world_sparse`, `world_normal_triplane`,
  and `world3d` all remain `continue` on R1 parity; K1 remains undecided; the
  encoding-redesign campaign remains a closed negative. `pixel2d` remains the
  only promoted representation. No code, model, or gate rule changed —
  `nrp/experiment_gate.py`'s −0.5 dB threshold, the `(8, 16, 24, 32, 40, 48)`
  look schedule, the 48-seed cap, and the α split are all unchanged.

## Seed budget: the decision this report hands to the user

Reaching the next scheduled look for R1 parity costs, at the measured rate of
one seed = 4 arms × 137 s ≈ 9.2 CPU-minutes (≈ 0.153 CPU-hours):

| target | seeds_needed | next scheduled look ≥ that | extra seeds beyond the 8 already run | est. cost |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `world_sparse` decidable | 17 | 24 | 16 | ≈ 2.4 CPU-hours |
| `world_normal_triplane` decidable | 14 | 16 | 8 | ≈ 1.2 CPU-hours |
| `world3d` decidable | 25 | 32 | 24 | ≈ 3.7 CPU-hours |
| all three decidable | 25 (max) | 32 | 24 | ≈ 3.7 CPU-hours |

(`seeds_needed` is a *total* seed count, not additional; only the six
pre-registered look points may be evaluated, so "16 needed" still means running
to look 16, and "25 needed" means running to look 32 — the next scheduled look
at or above 25.)

K1 and the encoding-redesign campaign have their own re-read evidence above but
no proposed next-look budget here: K1's own next step is gated on a decision
about K2–K4 funding, and the encoding-redesign campaign is closed, not
continuing.

**This report does not start, schedule, or recommend any of these runs.** Tasks
1–8 spent zero training; that boundary holds here. The question in front of the
user is which target, if any, is worth the CPU-hours above to move from
`continue`/undecided to a decidable verdict — that decision, and the
pre-registration it would need, is out of scope for this report.
