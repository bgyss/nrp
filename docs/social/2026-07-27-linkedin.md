I thought my first world-space Neural Render Proxy had regressed by 3.62 dB.

That conclusion was wrong. The experiment still failed, but for a much more
interesting reason.

I am extending my CPU-runnable reimplementation of Sancho et al.'s *Neural Render
Proxies for Interactive and Differentiable Lighting* (EGSR 2026). The paper's
network is tied to one camera through a 2D pixel-coordinate hashgrid. My first
representation-track question was deliberately narrower than “solve multi-view
neural rendering”: can I replace that 2D grid with a selectable 3D first-hit
world-position grid without losing more than 0.5 dB?

The toy scene passed. Country Kitchen appeared to fail catastrophically: 21.62 dB
for 3D versus a historical 25.24 dB 2D artifact.

Then I audited the baseline rather than accepting the headline. The historical
artifact drew validation lights from the training RNG after pool construction and
replacement; newer training uses a dedicated validation RNG. It also predates a
change to output initialization. None of the 12 held-out lights matched. On the
current fixed validation set, the historical model scores 23.27 dB—not 25.24 dB.
It is valid historical context, but it was not a controlled gate.

The binding comparison is the same-run pair:

- 2D pixel grid: 22.16 dB
- 3D world grid: 21.62 dB
- delta: −0.544 dB
- allowed loss: −0.5 dB

So the result remains an honest negative, but it misses by 0.044 dB rather than
3.62 dB.

I then ran two more paired seeds. The 3D deltas were −0.54, −0.77, and +0.25 dB.
Only one of three passes the unchanged gate. The mean delta is technically inside
the threshold, but averaging away individual failed runs would turn training
instability into a success claim. I am not doing that.

The diagnostics also changed my view of the likely mechanism. The observed weighted
hash collision fraction is about 23% for the 2D grid and 87% for the matched 3D
grid. But giving 3D 88% more parameters only reduces collisions to 67% and reaches
21.75 dB—still below the 2D control. More capacity is not a sufficient fix.

I tried a matched-budget, world-anchored tri-plane instead: independent XY, XZ, and
YZ multiresolution grids, concatenated at the MLP. It uses 106,239 parameters versus
106,085 for 2D (+0.15%) and reduces observed collisions to 35%. Its three paired
deltas were +1.36, −0.93, and −0.36 dB. That near-zero mean is encouraging, but the
variance is worse and one seed clearly misses. The candidate is not promoted.

Another useful finding: changing output initialization moves absolute seed-0 PSNR
by more than 2 dB for both representations, while their paired gap still fails.
This is not one clean collision bug. Representation, table allocation,
initialization, and stochastic training are interacting.

The possible way forward is now a bounded experiment ladder:

1. Five paired seeds crossing both initialization policies.
2. Matched-parameter table-allocation and tri-plane-fusion sweeps.
3. Rotated coordinate frames to test whether axis-aligned planes got lucky.
4. Error localization by surface normal, depth, light distance, and hash-slot load.
5. A second real scene only after one arm passes every seed.

The 0.5 dB gate stays binding. Multi-camera training stays blocked until a
world-anchored representation passes every controlled run on both real scenes.

The outcome I am after is not a screenshot from the best seed. It is a
representation reliable enough that the more expensive multi-camera question is
worth asking.
